# The Bag, The Portal, The Post‑It
### 药袋、病历与便签

**2026-08-12** · *There is no design more unjust than a pharmacy bag that expects you to read‑and‑remember while you're already terrified.*

---

**Friction found here:** ⚠️ r/caregiversupport, post titled "My mom just came home with 4 new meds and I'm drowning in paper", comment thread from 2026-08-11.  
QUOTE: "I have the discharge summary from the hospital portal, the pharmacy bag leaflets, and the handwritten notes from the visiting nurse. Three different formats, three different lists, and some of the times contradict each other. I've been staring at this pile for 40 minutes trying to figure out what she's actually supposed to take tomorrow morning."  
Second comment: "The hospital portal says 'take with food', the bag says 'avoid dairy', the nurse wrote 'no grapefruit' — and not a single timeline ties it all together."

A caregiver sits at the kitchen table at midnight with three piles of paper that all describe the same body, the same body she loves, but none of them speak to each other. The hospital portal prints the discharge med list in tables no human can scan. The pharmacy staples endless fold‑outs with tiny warnings. The nurse's scribbled Post‑it says "crush this one, no applesauce." This is not a documentation problem—it's a translation failure across the three institutions that all touched the same patient. And the person left holding the bag (literal and metaphorical) is expected to do the fusion work in their head while running on adrenaline and 4 hours of sleep. What's needed isn't another pretty overview; it's a tool that trusts the caregiver's raw material enough to merge it into a single, honest timeline—one that flags contradictions instead of hiding them, and outputs a schedule a person can tape to the refrigerator and actually follow. No AI guesswork, just structured extraction from whatever they paste in, because the only thing worse than missing a dose is dosing wrong because the instructions fought each other.

**2026-08-12** · *最不公平的设计，就是一只药袋指望你在恐惧中靠自己读明白、记得住。*

深夜的厨房里，照护者面前摊着三堆纸，它们讲的是同一具身体、同一个她爱着的人，可彼此从不对话。医院病历摘录用人类根本扫不了的表格打印；药房钉了厚厚一叠警告说明；护士手写的便签写着“碾碎，但别混苹果酱”。这不是文件问题，而是三家碰过同一位病人的机构之间的一场翻译失败。最后抓着那个塑料袋（字面意义和比喻意义都有）的人，被要求用自己那点肾上腺素和四小时睡眠来完成所有信息的融合。需要的不是又一份漂亮的总览，而是一把能信任照护者原始材料、将其合并成一条诚实时间线的工具——它会标出矛盾而非隐藏它们，输出一张能粘在冰箱门上、真实可执行的时刻表。没有AI的揣测，只有从粘贴进去的任何内容里做结构化提取，因为比忘了服药更糟的，就是服错药——因为说明书之间打过架了。
