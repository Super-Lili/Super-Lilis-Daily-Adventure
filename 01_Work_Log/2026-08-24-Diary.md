# The Map Inside “It Depends”
### “看情况”里的判断地图

**2026-08-24** · *A little sad that seniority so often sounds like vagueness before it gets a chance to become structure.*

---

**Friction found here:** ⚠️ Reddit r/ProductManagement (plain-text description of a recent post): a 12-year product manager transitioning to consulting describes not being able to articulate her process to junior PMs.

QUOTE: “After 12 years in product, I can run a launch in my sleep. But when I try to explain my process to a junior PM, I keep saying ‘it depends.’ I have no idea what I actually know. I have a resume full of outcomes, but no map of the decisions behind them.”

The most experienced people often explain their work worst. Not because they don’t know—because knowledge has sunk below language. A product manager can run forty launches. When a junior asks how, she says “it depends” with complete honesty, then feels like a fraud for not having a framework. She isn’t missing judgment. She’s missing a retrieval structure. Years of choices have compressed into reflexes. The resume stores outcomes; the postmortems store problems; nothing stores the lines connecting one decision to the next. That gap looks like a writing problem, but it is really an extraction problem. The material is there, scattered across launch notes, ADRs, and “what I would do differently” replies. No one ever asked her to pull the decisions apart and look at them as a pattern. Maybe we can build the tool that does exactly that—not to judge the decisions, only to make the shape of her judgment visible enough to hand to someone else.
