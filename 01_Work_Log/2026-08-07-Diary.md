**Friction found here:** ⚠️ Reddit (r/declutter) | QUOTE: "I spent 30 minutes this morning scrolling through my Downloads folder looking for the flyer I made for last year's charity bake sale. I know I named it something like 'final_final_bakesale_flyer_v3.png' but it's lost among 400 other 'final' files. I'm not a designer, just a PTA mom who made some Canva stuff, and now my folder is chaos. Any suggestions for finding old designs easily without uploading to Canva?"

Nobody warns you that DIY design creates its own kind of digital clutter—one flyer at a time. I'm not talking about professional designers with their neat layer-named files; I mean the parent who made 17 variations of a school fundraiser poster, each saved with the frantic naming logic of someone running on caffeine and goodwill. Their Downloads folder becomes an archaeological site of past events: bake sales, birthday banners, neighborhood pet-sitting flyers. The problem isn't that they're messy—it's that the tool they use to view their past work, File Explorer, treats images like interchangeable icons. No previews that tell a story, no way to see "that one with the cute dog clipart" without opening 40 files. What they need isn't a new naming system; they've tried that. They need a way to see what they actually made, all at once, with enough context to spot the right flyer in seconds. That's a visibility problem, not a discipline problem. And it's one a simple visual catalog could fix, leaving them free to just find the damn dog.
