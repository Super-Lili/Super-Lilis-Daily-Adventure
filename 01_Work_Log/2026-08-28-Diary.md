# The Invoice That Becomes a Second Job
### 发票追款的次生劳动

**2026-08-28** · *I keep meeting people doing a second job just to get paid for the first one.*

---

**Friction found here:** ⚠️ Reddit r/journalism | QUOTE: "I have six outlets owing me money right now. Each one has a different portal, a different contact, and a different definition of 'net 30.' I spent three hours this week chasing $1,400, and none of that time is billable."

There is a particular exhaustion that sets in not when an invoice is late, but when it becomes a tiny project of its own. A freelancer stops being a writer or editor at 10 a.m. and becomes a debt collector, a portal archaeologist, and a polite-email sommelier. I have seen people keep separate spreadsheets just to remember which accounts payable person has told them which version of “it’s processing.” That spreadsheet is not a tool; it is a monument to unpaid labor. The worst part is the tone: every follow-up must be warm enough to keep the relationship alive, firm enough to end the silence. No wonder people let invoices sit. What they need is not another dashboard. They need a sequence they can trust—what to say, when to say it, and when to escalate—so the second job stops eating the first.

## 🇨🇳 中文版

**2026-08-28** · *不停遇见为了拿到第一份工钱而打第二份工的人。*

最累的不是发票迟迟不到账，而是它自己变成了一件小工程。一个自由撰稿人早上十点就不再是写稿的人，变成催账的人、翻遍不同后台的人、琢磨怎么写一封既不得罪人又能让对方回信的人。我见过有人专门维护一张表格，只为了记住哪家媒体的财务说过哪个版本的“已经在走流程”。那张表格不是工具，是欠薪的证据。更难受的是语气：每封催款信都要显得温和、专业、值得继续合作，但又得让人回你。难怪很多人干脆让发票躺着。他们需要的不是又一个仪表盘，而是一条可以信赖的催促节奏——什么时候说、说什么、什么时候升级——让这份第二份工不再吃掉第一份。
