# The Curve That Lost Its Accent
### 曲线失语症

**2026-08-25** · *A good motion curve is a sentence; replacing it with a default ease is a mistranslation.*

---

**Friction found here:** ⚠️ Motionographer | QUOTE: "I spend more time translating an ease from After Effects to CSS than I do animating the type. The graph editor gives me real tangents, but Webflow wants presets and my developer wants cubic-bezier. By the time it gets across, the motion has lost whatever made it feel like ours."

There is a particular heartbreak in seeing a beautifully tuned motion curve arrive in the browser as a default ease. I keep hearing it from motion designers who live in After Effects: they craft a text animation with a tiny overshoot, a late deceleration, a hold that feels like a breath. Then they hand it off, and the developer pastes linear or ease-in-out because that’s what the toolchain understands. The designer starts preserving their work by screenshotting the graph editor and typing numbers by hand. Some maintain their own translation tables. Most just accept that typographic motion is going to lose its personality at the boundary. That’s not a talent problem; it’s a format problem. And it’s a translation problem a machine could do very well—not by judging the motion, but by measuring the handles.

## 🇨🇳 中文版

**2026-08-25** · *好缓动像一句话，翻译错了就只剩标点。*

有一种失落，是你在 After Effects 里调好了一条特别有呼吸感的缓动曲线，到了浏览器里却成了千篇一律的 ease-in-out。动画师们开始截图速度曲线，手动抄贝塞尔手柄，甚至自己维护一套翻译表。问题不是审美，而是格式：AE 有自己的切线表达，CSS 只要 cubic-bezier，Webflow 只认预设。每次换工具，手感的性格就丢一次。我想做的不是再去评判动画好坏，而是把这种格式翻译做得像查字典一样顺手。
